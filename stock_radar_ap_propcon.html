    1 · Business problem statement
    
  
    AP (Thailand) PCL is one of Thailand’s largest listed residential developers — a low-rise-led (townhouse / single-detached) model with condominiums developed largely through joint ventures — and a meaningful constituent of the SET PROPCON (Property & Construction) industry index. Its daily price is a blend of four forces that a portfolio manager needs to separate: market beta (SET), sector rotation (PROPCON vs SET), rates and credit conditions (Thai and US yields acting through mortgage affordability, bank rejection rates and cost of capital), and flows and sentiment (foreign net buying, participation).
    
  
    Why daily, and why it matters:
    
  
      
    
    • Portfolio management — decomposing each day’s move into systematic vs idiosyncratic shows whether active risk in AP is being paid for (stock-specific alpha) or is an unintended macro/rate bet that could be hedged more cheaply via the index or sector.
      • 
    
    • Risk mitigation — residential developers behave like long-duration, rate-sensitive assets with negatively skewed drawdowns in risk-off episodes. A daily regime signal and rolling sensitivities flag when exposure should be cut or hedged before a drawdown compounds.
      • 
    
    • Alpha generation — large residuals (moves unexplained by factors) mark news or flow dislocations that tend to partially revert or trend; regime timing can improve risk-adjusted returns versus static holding.
      • 
  
    
  
    The decision question the tool answers each morning: “What moved AP yesterday, how much of it was systematic, what would a plausible shock do to price and market cap, and is the risk regime turning?”

    4 · Interactive dashboard prototype
    
  
    This file is the prototype: a single, dependency-free HTML/CSS/JavaScript application (no CDN, no server) that runs offline or on localhost. Architecture:
    
  
      
    
    • Data layer — CSV ingestion with header auto-mapping, Thai BE date handling, forward-fill (≤3 days, never back-fill), and data-quality checks (coverage, stale prices, 6σ outliers, calendar gaps).
      • 
    
    • Attribution engine — rolling OLS (default 120 days) of AP daily returns on same-day factors; standardised βs feed the radar; βs × σ convert into % / THB / market-cap impacts and drive the scenario simulator.
      • 
    
    • Regime engine — walk-forward, purged and embargoed L2-logistic model producing an out-of-sample P(Bear); EMA smoothing; cost-based threshold with hysteresis; backtest with execution lag and transaction costs.
      • 
    
    • Presentation — high-contrast dark/light themes, colour-blind-validated palette, every chart hoverable, status never encoded by colour alone (icon + label).
      • 
  
    
  
    Production path: schedule the Python helper (Data tab) after the SET close (~17:30 ICT), write the CSV to a shared folder, and open this page — or port the engine to Python/Streamlit with a database when multiple users or intraday data are needed.
    

  
    5 · Regime classification — Bull vs Bear signal
    
  
    Step 1 — Define the event (label)
    
  
    yₜ = 1[ ln(P₍ₜ₊₁₊ₕ₎ / P₍ₜ₊₁₎) < −k · σ̂ₜ · √h ]       (Bear = 1)

h = 10 trading days, k = 0.5, σ̂ₜ = 20-day st.dev. of daily log returns known at close t.
The window starts at t+1 because a signal formed at close t can only be traded at t+1.
    
  
    Scaling by σ̂ means “Bear” is a meaningful decline relative to current volatility, not noise; with k = 0.5 the base rate is roughly 30–40%.
    
  
    Step 2 — Convert features into a probability
    
  
    zⱼ,ₜ = clip( (xⱼ,ₜ − μⱼ,train) / σⱼ,train , −5, 5 )
P(Bear)ₜ = σ( w₀ + Σⱼ wⱼ zⱼ,ₜ ),   σ(u) = 1 / (1 + e⁻ᵘ)
ŵ = argmin  −Σ [ y log p + (1−y) log(1−p) ] + λ₂ Σⱼ≥₁ wⱼ²      (λ₂ = 20, intercept unpenalised)
    
  
    Walk-forward: refit every 20 days on an expanding window. A model used on day b is trained only on t ≤ b − 1 − h − e (purge h for the overlapping label, plus embargo e = 5 days). Smoothing: P̃ₜ = α·Pₜ + (1 − α)·P̃ₜ₋₁ with α = 2/(s+1), s = 3.
    
  
    Step 3 — Cost-based threshold
    
  
    Declare Bear  ⇔  P̃ · C_FN  >  (1 − P̃) · C_FP   ⇔   P̃ ≥ θ* = C_FP / (C_FP + C_FN) = 1 / (1 + λ),   λ = C_FN / C_FP

C_FN = cost of staying long into a Bear (expected drawdown)
C_FP = cost of a false alarm (missed upside + round-trip costs + forgone dividend carry)

Default λ = 1.5  →  θ* = 0.40
Hysteresis b = 0.05:  enter Bear when P̃ ≥ θ* + b = 0.45 ;  return to Bull when P̃ ≤ θ* − b = 0.35
    
  
    Risk-management rationale for the cut-off
    
  
      
    
    • Asymmetric loss (λ > 1). Losses compound (−20% needs +25% to recover), Thai property names show negatively skewed, liquidity-gapped sell-offs, and long-only mandates are judged on drawdown. The dashboard also reports a data-implied λ̂ = E[loss | Bear] / E[gain | not Bear, gain > 0] from pre-out-of-sample history as a sanity check — currently —.
      • 
    
    • Above the base rate. θ* = 0.40 sits slightly above the typical Bear base rate, so the model must show evidence beyond the prior before de-risking.
      • 
    
    • Hysteresis cuts whipsaw. A ±0.05 band and EMA smoothing reduce flip-flopping that would otherwise be eaten by ~15 bp/side trading costs.
      • 
    
    • Set ex-ante, not optimised. λ and b are policy choices fixed before testing; optimising them on the backtest would overfit. Change λ to reflect your mandate (e.g. λ = 2 → θ* = 0.33 for a capital-preservation book; λ = 1 → θ* = 0.50 for a benchmark-relative book).
      • 
    
    • Action mapping. Bear → reduce to underweight / hedge with SET50 futures; Bull → hold target weight. A continuous alternative is size = target × (1 − P̃), capped by vol-target limits.

    Ethical guardrails
    
  
      
    
    • Decision support, human in the loop. The signal informs a PM’s judgement; it does not place orders. Any automation needs separate approval, limits and a kill-switch.
      • 
    
    • Not investment advice. If outputs are shared with clients, check with compliance whether this constitutes investment advice requiring licensing under Thai SEC rules, and apply suitability and fair-treatment standards.
      • 
    
    • Market integrity. No material non-public information may be loaded; information barriers and restricted lists apply. Do not broadcast signals on a mid-cap in a way that could move the price or front-run client orders.
      • 
    
    • Conflicts of interest. Disclose house positions in AP or related underwriting/advisory relationships when outputs are distributed.
      • 
    
    • Data rights & privacy. Use licensed market data within its terms (Yahoo Finance is for personal use); no personal data is processed, so PDPA exposure is minimal, but keep access logs.
      • 
  
    
  
    Interface transparency disclosures (implemented)
    
  
      
    
    • Persistent banner stating data source (synthetic vs user CSV), as-of date and “not investment advice”.
      • 
    
    • Signal shown as a probability with its out-of-sample AUC / Brier skill beside it, and an explicit low-skill flag.
      • 
    
    • Model card: version, parameters, training window, refit count, OOS sample size.
      • 
    
    • Explainability: today’s factor decomposition and per-feature contributions to P(Bear).
      • 
    
    • Backtest labelled price-only, gross of dividends, with execution lag and costs stated.
      • 
  
    
  
    Lifecycle
    
  
    Three lines: developer documents and tests → independent validator reproduces results and challenges assumptions → users sign off on intended use and limits. Version every change to data, features, parameters and code; re-validate annually or on any material change.
